ARISE needs a major, coherent visual upgrade across the app, not another simple blue card, a color-only change, or a one-screen redesign. Use original RPG/System artwork, dramatic typography, metallic/angular HUD frames, and three layers: atmospheric background, large artwork/emblem, and readable foreground controls.

Different screens should have different visual identities while remaining part of the same ARISE System. Include original character/Guardian artwork, crystals, rank badges, item artwork, environmental backgrounds, particles, and energy effects; the result should feel like a premium futuristic RPG System app rather than a normal study dashboard.

**Why:** The user says the current UI is too simple and repeatedly uses dark navy plus cyan/blue cards.

**How to apply:** Use deep black/navy/indigo environments with controlled contextual accents: cyan/electric blue for System and Maths; violet/magenta for NCERT/vocabulary; purple/indigo for Reasoning; teal/cyan for English; crimson/orange for Recovery; gold/emerald for Success; violet/electric blue for Gates; crimson/purple/black for Boss; and dark metallic inventory with rarity colors. Do not make every screen blue, cyan, or purple.

References are inspiration only, never copied copyrighted characters, logos, screenshots, or anime art. Preserve all progression, rewards, inventory, equipment, persistence, and history. Keep mobile safe areas, touch targets, lightweight Android animations, and reduced-motion behavior.

**Why:** The user explicitly asks for a visual/UX overhaul without breaking functionality and rejects visual-completion claims based on tests alone.

**How to apply:** Inspect actual mobile screens and event states before calling the visual overhaul complete. Life Potion remains a fictional in-app item with no medicine or physical reward claims.

Content first, artwork second: do not place high-detail artwork behind small text. Use designated hero/art regions and dark content surfaces. The final master brief requests one coherent functional and visual pass, without separate feature approvals.

**Why:** The user explicitly reported that background artwork was interfering with readability and said not to stop after visual changes.

**How to apply:** Reuse working systems, fill genuine functional gaps, and verify text contrast and connected study/reward flows rather than treating decorative changes as completion.
